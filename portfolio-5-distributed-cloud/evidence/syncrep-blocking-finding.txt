FINDING: statement_timeout does NOT interrupt a SyncRep wait
==============================================================
Captured while Experiment 3 was blocked. The backend had already
run SET statement_timeout = '10s' and had been waiting far longer
than 10 seconds.

WHY. Once a transaction has written its commit record and entered
the SyncRep wait, it is ALREADY DURABLE LOCALLY. statement_timeout
governs statement execution, not the post-commit replication wait,
so it cannot fire here. PostgreSQL documents this: the wait is
interruptible only by a client cancel or backend termination, and
even then the transaction REMAINS COMMITTED on the primary while
the client never received an acknowledgement.

That is a worse failure mode than plain unavailability: the
transaction is in limbo. The client must assume it failed; the
primary has already applied it. For a payment platform that is the
classic double-submit hazard, and it is why idempotency keys exist
(see portfolio-4-nosql metadata.api.idempotency_key).
