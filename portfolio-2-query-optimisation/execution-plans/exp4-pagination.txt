4B - keyset pagination instead of OFFSET
==============================================================
Keyset ('seek') pagination replaces OFFSET with a predicate on the last row
seen. The index can jump straight to the position, so cost stays flat no
matter how deep the page.

==============================================================
4C - CORRECTION: the keyset cursor needs a tie-breaker
==============================================================
The keyset query in 4B used a single-column cursor:

    WHERE account_id = :a AND posted_at < :last_seen
    ORDER BY posted_at DESC LIMIT 50

That is WRONG when two postings share a timestamp, which this dataset does:
4 (account, posted_at) groups contain more than one row, the worst holding 6.
Both sides of a transfer are posted with the same settled_at, so collisions
are structural, not accidental.

With a strict '<' the page boundary SKIPS every tied row. With '<=' it
REPEATS them. Either way the user sees a corrupted statement.

Demonstrated on account 1 at timestamp 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01

--- rows sharing that timestamp ---
 entry_id | direction |   amount    |           posted_at           
----------+-----------+-------------+-------------------------------
        1 | DEBIT     |  50000.0000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
        3 | DEBIT     | 120000.0000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
        5 | DEBIT     |  75500.5000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
       11 | CREDIT    |  20000.0000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
       12 | DEBIT     |  10000.0000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
       15 | CREDIT    |  10000.0000 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
(6 rows)

--- BROKEN cursor: strict < on posted_at alone skips the tied rows ---
 entry_id |           posted_at           
----------+-------------------------------
   269174 | 2026-11-01 00:58:04.954317+01
   136009 | 2026-11-01 00:56:39.660435+01

   338927 | 2026-11-01 00:32:37.904762+01
   104960 | 2026-11-01 00:01:26.572091+01
(5 rows)

--- CORRECT cursor: row-value comparison on (posted_at, entry_id) ---
 entry_id |           posted_at           
----------+-------------------------------
       12 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
       11 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
        5 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
        3 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
        1 | 2026-11-13 09:04:12.926938+01
(5 rows)


The row-value comparison (posted_at, entry_id) < (:ts, :id) resumes exactly
where the previous page ended, including partway through a tied group.
PostgreSQL compares the tuple lexicographically and can still use a B-tree
index on the same column order.
